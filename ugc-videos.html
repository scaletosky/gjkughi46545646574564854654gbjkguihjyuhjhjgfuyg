WHAT IS UGC?

UGC, in plain terms

UGC stands for user-generated content. In brand marketing, "UGC" or "UGC-style" video usually describes content presented in a creator or customer-like format — natural delivery, direct-to-camera presentation, product use in a real setting, personal experience, demonstrations, problem/solution storytelling and a conversational tone — rather than a traditional, highly polished brand advertisement.

It's worth being precise about one thing: not every UGC video is literally filmed by an actual paying customer. Most brand UGC is creator-produced UGC-style content — a creator is briefed and paid to make a video that looks and feels like organic, customer-style content. That's different from genuine customer-generated content, which comes from real buyers with no brief involved. Both have their place, but we're upfront about which one a project is producing, since that distinction matters for how the content should be described and used.

UGC VS INFLUENCER MARKETING

Two different things that often get confused

UGC and influencer marketing can overlap, but they're solving different problems.

UGC is about...

  • The content itself, as a brand-owned creative asset
  • Creator-style presentation built around a brief
  • Product or service communication
  • Use across your own social and paid placements

Influencer marketing is about...

  • The creator's own audience and reach
  • Distribution through that creator's channel
  • Sponsored promotion to their followers
  • Influence, more than the video itself

A creator doesn't need a large personal following to produce useful UGC — for UGC, the value is usually in the content itself, not the audience it was filmed for. We don't make ownership or usage-rights claims here; those are confirmed per project based on the agreed creator arrangement.

UGC VS TRADITIONAL BRAND CONTENT

Neither is automatically better

Traditional brand content is often...

  • Polished and highly produced
  • Fully brand-directed
  • Controlled in look, lighting and pacing
  • Built for brand films, product shoots or corporate video

UGC-style content is often...

  • Conversational and creator-led
  • Natural, direct-to-camera
  • Relatable rather than polished
  • Built for feeds, stories and ad placements

Most brands end up using both, for different purposes. If your project needs professional, planned production — brand films, product shoots, corporate video — that's covered under Video Shoot & Editing instead.

UGC STRATEGY

UGC built around a purpose, not just a format

Before any filming, we define the audience, the product, the key message, the platform and the funnel stage — since each of those shapes what the content should actually do.

AwarenessConsiderationConversionRetargeting

Awareness content introduces a product or a problem to people who don't know the brand yet. Consideration content explains how the product actually works. Conversion content presents a benefit, use case or offer clearly enough to prompt action. Retargeting content addresses objections and hesitation for people who've already shown interest. Not every campaign needs all four stages running at once — which ones matter depends on the brand and the objective.

HOOKS

What a hook actually does

A hook is the first few seconds of a video — its job is to earn the next few seconds of attention, not to promise anything about how the video will perform after that.

Useful hook structures include identifying a problem the viewer recognizes, asking a relevant question, introducing an unexpected benefit, showing the product immediately, addressing a common objection up front, or demonstrating a result or process without making unsupported performance claims. We build hooks around what the audience actually cares about — we don't promise that any specific hook will go viral, since that depends on far more than the video itself.

SCRIPTS & BRIEFS

Structure without sounding scripted

A good UGC brief gives a creator enough direction to stay on-message, while leaving room for natural, unscripted delivery.

A typical brief covers the objective, target audience, key message, hook, talking points, product or service details, CTA, tone, visual direction, do/don't guidance and the required format. Creators work from this structure rather than a rigid word-for-word script, so the delivery still feels like a real person talking rather than reading lines.

PRODUCTION

How the content actually gets filmed

Production stays focused on natural, creator-led presentation rather than a full studio setup.

Filming works from the brief — environment, framing, lighting and audio are kept simple and natural rather than heavily produced, since an overly polished look works against what UGC is meant to feel like. Creators are guided through the required shots and typically deliver a few takes so there's usable footage to edit from. Where raw footage is included in the agreed deliverables, it can be provided for the brand's own editing or future creative use.

EDITING & VARIATIONS

Editing that keeps the natural feel

UGC editing is lighter-touch than traditional brand editing — the goal is to keep it feeling native, not turn it into a polished commercial.

01Trimming & pacing

04Platform formatting & aspect ratios
05CTA overlays
06Multiple hooks & CTA variations

One core concept can often be adapted into a few variations — different hooks, different CTAs — useful for creative testing across audiences or funnel stages. We don't promise a fixed number of variations upfront; that's defined as part of the project scope.

UGC FOR META ADS

Why UGC often shows up in paid social

UGC-style creative tends to look native inside a feed rather than like an obvious ad, which is part of why it's a common format for paid social. It also gives a campaign a creator's-eye perspective, multiple angles and hooks to test, and a natural way to handle product demonstrations and objections within an ad. That said, UGC doesn't guarantee lower CPA or higher ROAS — ad performance still comes down to the offer, audience, campaign setup, landing page, tracking, market and budget, with creative as one part of that mix. This connects directly with our Meta Ads work when UGC is being produced specifically for a paid campaign.

WHAT OFTEN GOES WRONG

Common UGC mistakes

01

Treating UGC Like a Commercial

Over-polishing a UGC video defeats the point — the natural feel is what makes it work.

02

Choosing a Creator on Looks Alone

Style, tone and delivery matter more than appearance for whether the content actually lands.

03

No Clear Hook

Without a hook, a video loses attention before the message even gets across.

04

Overly Rigid Scripts

Word-for-word scripts often read as scripted on camera — a brief works better than a script.

05

Only One Creative Angle

Repeating the same angle limits what a campaign can learn from testing.

06

Forgetting Captions or the CTA

Most viewers watch on mute and need a clear next step — both are easy to overlook.

07

Assuming UGC Guarantees Performance

UGC is a creative format, not a guarantee — offer, audience and campaign setup still decide the outcome.

08

Unclear Deliverables & Usage Terms

Confirming quantities and usage rights upfront avoids confusion later.
